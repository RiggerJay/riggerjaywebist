Consent, touch, and artistic practice

Consent & touch in photography

I am not in a professional photographer, very rarely do I ever earn any income out of my work. If I was to add up every penny I have ever earned in years of doing photography, I would not have even paid for my first camera body (I’m on my 4th).

I have my own style, and presume you have looked at my photos before you decided to you want to shoot with me.

When I reach out to someone and ask to shoot... I mean just that, being behind the camera taking photos, maybe putting some rope on but that is all talked about before hand.

Just to say it again, asking you to shoot, is not asking you to play or fuck. I want to make art, and keep on reading.

So many use photography/rope as a way to get in the pants of their model, that is not my goal, I just really like making art.

I have been more and more doing work for friends’ OnlyFans type sites and hope to do more of that.

What do I do with my photos?

I like to show them off.

  • Social networks (Fetlife, Reddit, X/Twitter, Instagram, etc.)
  • Self promotion (fliers, event programs)
  • Art (prints, art shows)
  • Book? (a one day goal)

Down to business

Pre-shoot

  • Talk with them, understand what story they want to convey
  • Set up expectations
  • Set up details to be used in model releases (ie no face or such)
  • Set expectation of side content during shot I might want to take for myself

During Shoot

  • Sign model releases/Content release etc.
  • Follow what we discussed pre-shoot, and don't renegotiate anything; can always meet up another time
  • Don’t be a guy with camera
  • Have film/media in camera (doh!)

Post-Shoot

  • Review and create a selection of photos that I feel capture the story client was looking for
  • Get a “top 10” list of favorites from client
  • Within pre-negotiated time span, get edited final version of the “top 10” list plus others I liked to client

Of course there are tons of other nuances, but this is the high level take on it.

Most important, I strive not to be this guy.
